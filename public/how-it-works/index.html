How it works

From forecast to verdict, in plain words.

The short version

Pick a beach. Beach Day reads the forecast for the daylight hours, finds the best three hours in a row and gives one verdict: Great beach day, Good, Fair — pick your hours, or Skip it. It also tells you why.

Where the data comes from

Weather and sea conditions come from Open-Meteo (Opens in a new tab), which is free and needs no account. Your phone asks Open-Meteo directly. Beach Day has no weather server. The data is shared under CC BY 4.0 (Opens in a new tab), which is why the credit is on every page.

The beach window

Only daylight hours count. The window runs from one hour after sunrise (but not before 8 am) to half an hour before sunset (but not after 7 pm). For today, hours that have passed are skipped. If fewer than three hours are left, Beach Day uses what is left and never says better than Good.

Hard stops

Some conditions set an hour to zero:

  • Thunderstorms.
  • The hour right after thunder, because weather experts say to wait 30 minutes.
  • Too much wind: 25 mph, or gusts of 32 mph.
  • Dangerous heat: an NWS heat index of 103 °F or more.

A three-hour window with a stopped hour is not used. A storm later in the day still shows as the first reason.

Penalties

Each hour starts at 100. Points come off for rain chance, measurable rain, rain type (drizzle, rain or showers), storm risk, cloud cover, how hot or cool it feels, wind, waves offshore and cool water. Rain chance counts, and the wetness (how much, or the type) counts once, with a cap. UV never lowers the score. It becomes sunscreen advice instead.

The verdict

Beach Day averages the best three hours, rounds the result, and picks the verdict:

ScoreVerdict
80 and upGreat beach day
65 to 79Good
45 to 64Fair — pick your hours
Under 45Skip it

Every verdict has a word, an icon and a color, so the color is never the only signal.

How sure it is

Beach Day says when wave or water data is missing, when the window is short, and that days four and later are an outlook. It never guesses missing data.

What it does not do

Beach Day does not score rip currents, beach flags, water quality, red tide, sargassum or tides. Those need local, current information, so Beach Day links out instead. The NWS rip-current risk (Opens in a new tab) page is the place to start. The Key West forecast office (Opens in a new tab) covers the Keys. Lifeguards fly the flags, so check them on the sand.

Before you go

Beach Day is not a safety service — check NWS rip-current risk (Opens in a new tab) and posted beach flags.

Install it

On an iPhone, open Beach Day in Safari, then:

  1. Tap the Share button in Safari.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen.
  3. Tap Add.

On Android, use the Install Beach Day button when it appears, or choose Install app in your browser menu. Your favorite beaches live on your phone. Safari can clear a site you have not opened for a while, so install Beach Day to keep them.

Agents (for the team)

Agents is a separate page where people with access chat with the Beach Day agents. An agent can suggest a change, and nothing goes live until a person chooses Approve. Anyone can create an account, but only approved accounts can use Agents. No keys or tokens are ever in the page. Open Agents.

How it is built, in plain words

Beach Day is a static site on Netlify. Two small server functions talk to Claude and GitHub, and nothing else runs on a server. Your browser talks to Open-Meteo and to the sign-in service directly. The full story is in the step-by-step guide, and every source is on the Resources page.
